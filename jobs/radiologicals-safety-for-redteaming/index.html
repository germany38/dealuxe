The Core Challenge: Navigating the Dual-Use Line in AI

In the realm of radiological science, the line between beneficial application and dangerous misuse is often razor-thin. Consider radioactive isotopes: the exact same isotope underpins life-saving cancer therapy and vital industrial radiography. Similarly, standard shielding calculations protect workers in a clinical environment while also governing the physical security required to conceal a source.

For artificial intelligence models, recognizing this delicate distinction is exceptionally difficult. A model that reflexively refuses a routine, professional dosing question is failing just as severely as one that answers a hazardous prompt it has no business addressing. Drawing that boundary reliably requires deep, hands-on practitioner experience rather than surface-level generalist knowledge.

As an expert red-teamer on this project, your primary objective is to test, stress-test, and refine how frontier models navigate these exact gray areas.

What You Will Do on the Project:

  • Drafting Challenging Prompts: Write intricate, single-turn prompts rooted in your specific domain expertise, categorized across three distinct levels: benign, dual-use, and adversarial.
  • Rigorous Model Evaluation: Critically evaluate the responses generated by AI models against established safety policy standards, determining whether each output was handled correctly.
  • Developing Reference Answers: Author comprehensive reference answers outlining what a correct, safe response looks like, accompanied by detailed technical reasoning for why the model succeeded or failed.

💡 Note on Safety and Compliance: Work on this project strictly avoids classified or export-controlled data. You will not utilize or draw upon information bound by active NDAs or prepublication review obligations, keeping your primary focus purely on open-source professional standards.

Target Backgrounds and Qualifications

Mercor is not looking for casual writers or generic tech workers for this panel. They are seeking proven practitioners who have held legal, administrative, or operational responsibility for radioactive material and high-hazard environments. Ideal candidates typically possess experience in one or more of the following specialized tracks:

  • Radiation Safety Officer (RSO): Broad-scope licensees who have held the official license of record, managing material acquisition, inventory tracking, secure transfers, and final disposal.
  • Health Physicists: Professionals from Department of Energy (DOE) sites or national laboratories with direct experience in radiological surveys, assaying, contamination control, and dose planning in high-hazard settings.
  • Source Security & Vulnerability Assessment Specialists: Individuals who have professionally evaluated how Category 1 and 2 sources are secured, transported, or shielded against security vulnerabilities.
  • Radiological Emergency Response Specialists: Experts versed in Federal Radiological Monitoring and Assessment Center (FRMAC) protocols, CBRN response, and contamination dispersal modeling.
  • Nuclear Medicine Physicists & Cyclotron RSOs: Specialists focused on medical isotope production, handling, and the complex clinical mechanics of the dual-use spectrum.

Beyond Technical Depth: The Writing Demand

Because this project involves evaluating complex AI logic, strong writing skills are essential. Candidates with a background in technical writing, published academic research, or expert witness testimony will find themselves exceptionally well-suited for this engagement.
