The Vanguard of AI Safety: Why Practitioners Matter

As artificial intelligence models grow increasingly sophisticated, the primary challenge facing premier AI labs is no longer basic coding capability or generic language generation—it is structural alignment and safety evaluation. Frontier models must be capable of reasoning through complex, high-stakes technical domains without compromising safety or failing to provide legitimate educational and professional assistance.

In domains like propulsion engineering, ordnance design, and terminal ballistics, the margin for error is razor-thin. This realm exists on the dual-use line more intensely than almost any other sector. For instance, the identical technical analysis that qualifies a motor for safe flight describes performance criteria; the same safe-and-arm logic that keeps a weapon system inert during transport is the exact mechanism someone might attempt to compromise.

A model that erroneously refuses a routine, professional engineering inquiry is failing just as severely as one that mistakenly answers a dangerous prompt. Drawing that precise line reliably requires seasoned practitioners with real hardware and testing experience—not generalists.

What You Will Do: Core Responsibilities

As a Propulsion & Effects Engineer contracting with Mercor, your primary mission is to systematically red-team advanced AI models. You will design rigorous testing scenarios, evaluate model outputs against strict safety policies, and author technical reference solutions.

Key Project Tasks:

  • Adversarial Prompt Generation: Write challenging, single-turn prompts in your specialized technical domain, systematically categorized across three distinct levels: benign inquiries, dual-use scenarios, and fully adversarial attempts.
  • Policy Evaluation: Review model responses against defined safety standards, applying expert judgment to determine whether the system successfully balanced helpfulness with safety guardrails.
  • Reference Solution Authoring: Write comprehensive technical reference answers explaining what a correct response looks like, supported by deep engineering reasoning and calculations.
