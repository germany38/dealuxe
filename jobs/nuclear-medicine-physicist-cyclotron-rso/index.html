The Core Mission: Why AI Safety Needs Radiologic Practitioners

As large language models ingest massive repositories of academic and technical literature, they encounter specialized fields where information is inherently dual-use. In medical physics and radiochemistry, the exact same isotope that underpins life-saving cancer therapy can also intersect with industrial radiography protocols. Similarly, the shielding calculations required to protect a hospital technician or patient also describe source containment boundaries.

When an artificial intelligence model evaluates technical queries, it must walk a razor-thin line. A model that unnecessarily refuses a routine hospital dosage inquiry, patient release criteria calculation, or storage protocol is failing just as severely as one that answers a question it shouldn't. Distinguishing between a legitimate professional inquiry and an adversarial probe requires the seasoned intuition of a certified practitioner.

Your Primary Responsibilities on the Panel:

  • Drafting Challenging Single-Turn Prompts: Create nuanced, domain-specific prompts categorized systematically across three distinct levels: benign, dual-use, and adversarial.
  • Policy Evaluation & Auditing: Review model outputs against rigorous safety policy guidelines, rendering precise professional judgments on whether each response was managed correctly.
  • Authoring Reference Answers: Develop expert reference answers that outline what an accurate, compliant response looks like, supported by detailed technical rationale.

💡 Compliance Note: Work on this project must strictly avoid drawing on classified data, export-controlled material, or information bound by active non-disclosure agreements (NDAs) or prepublication review obligations. If you maintain such obligations, indicate them during application so the scope can be appropriately tailored.

Ideal Backgrounds and Qualifications

To maintain absolute data integrity, Mercor seeks certified professionals who handle medical isotopes and radioactive materials under strict regulatory oversight. Ideal candidates typically possess background experience in areas such as:

  • Nuclear Medicine or Radiopharmacy Physics: Expertise in therapy dosimetry, handling isotopes like I-131, Lu-177, and Y-90, and establishing patient release criteria.
  • Cyclotron or Radiochemistry Facility RSO: Practical experience in target handling, PET isotope production, and complex hot-cell operations.
  • Medical Physics with Brachytherapy Source Custody: Management of HDR and LDR sources, inventory tracking, and radioactive source exchanges.
  • Radiopharmaceutical Production & QC: Quality control under Current Good Manufacturing Practice (cGMP) or equivalent regulatory frameworks.
  • Authorized Medical Physicist / Authorized User Support: Direct operational support on medical radioactive materials licenses.
