Detailed Job Overview & Responsibilities

Designed for highly accomplished researchers, this role bridges theoretical life sciences with cutting-edge artificial intelligence engineering. As a Molecular Biology Subject-Matter Expert (SME), your primary mission is to ensure that AI models reason accurately, rigorously, and safely when processing intricate biological data.

Key Responsibilities:

  • AI Reasoning Optimization: Partner directly with research and engineering teams to identify precise breakdowns in a model's molecular biology logic, systematically closing capability gaps.
  • Challenging Task Design: Construct rigorous, domain-relevant tasks and write comprehensive, publication-grade solutions that serve as absolute ground truth. This spans designing primers for PCR, qPCR, and cloning; creating plasmids and expression vectors; engineering gRNA and sgRNA for CRISPR editing; and formulating mRNA constructs and HDR repair templates.
  • Output Auditing & Feedback: Review complex AI outputs against expert solutions, providing detailed, structured written critiques regarding scientific correctness, biological viability, and experimental rigor.
  • Rubric & Guideline Authoring: Establish internal standards and evaluation metrics covering guide and target selection, homology arm length, primer melting temperature (Tm) and specificity, codon optimization, and regulatory elements.
  • Cross-Functional Collaboration: Align closely with fellow scientific SMEs to guarantee complete consistency and empirical accuracy across all AI training datasets.

Candidate Qualifications: What It Takes to Excel

Because these generative AI models power critical scientific exploration, the standard for admission is intentionally rigorous. Candidates must showcase deep, hands-on empirical experience and academic pedigree.

Core Qualifications & Requirements:
  • Advanced Academic Background: An advanced degree—with a PhD strongly preferred—in molecular biology, genetics, biochemistry, synthetic biology, bioengineering, or a closely related life science field.
  • Hands-on Construct Design: Proven, practical experience designing nucleic acid constructs prior to physical experimentation. This includes primers, plasmids and vectors, gRNA/sgRNA, mRNA, and HDR templates, alongside mastery of underlying cloning methods (Gibson, Golden Gate, Gateway, restriction enzyme cloning) and codon optimization.
  • Distinguished Publication Record: A strong peer-reviewed publication history, heavily weighted toward first-author contributions in prominent journals such as Nature and the Nature family, Cell, eLife, Nucleic Acids Research, PNAS, and the EMBO Journal (applicants are requested to include publication links).
  • Availability & Commitment: The ability to reliably commit to at least 20 hours per week during regular weekdays.
  • Communication Skills: Exceptional written English communication, with the precise ability to articulate complex biochemical reasoning behind every design choice.
