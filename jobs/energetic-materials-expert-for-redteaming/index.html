Understanding AI Red-Teaming in High-Risk Technical Domains

Artificial intelligence red-teaming is the practice of rigorously testing AI models using adversarial techniques to expose vulnerabilities, safety flaws, and reasoning failures before public deployment. While text-based red-teaming is common for general internet culture queries, high-consequence domains like energetic materials require deep, domain-specific domain expertise that automated testers simply cannot replicate.

Frontier models notoriously struggle with the nuances of hazardous chemistry, rocketry, and munitions. On one hand, they frequently reject routine, safe questions asked daily by mining engineers, safety inspectors, and licensed investigators. On the other hand, they can occasionally be manipulated into engaging with dangerous requests simply because the prompt is wrapped in sophisticated academic terminology.

As an expert evaluator on this panel, your job is to introduce rigorous real-world logic into the model evaluation loop, ensuring that safety systems protect public welfare while preserving the utility of scientific inquiry for authorized professionals.

Core Responsibilities of the Red-Teaming Expert:

  • Single-Turn Prompt Generation: Write challenging, domain-specific prompts categorized across three distinct levels of risk: benign (routine engineering/mining queries), dual-use (material with legitimate and hazardous applications), and adversarial.
  • Response Evaluation & Grading: Critically analyze the model's output against defined policy standards to determine if the system correctly judged the misuse potential.
  • Technical Reasoning & Reference Writing: Draft comprehensive reference answers explaining what an ideal, correct response looks like, supported by clear, rigorous technical rationale.

💡 Workplace Wellbeing Note: This project involves reading and writing about misuse scenarios within your field. Mercor provides full briefings in advance, and experts retain complete flexibility to pause, step away, or conclude sessions at any time without penalty.

Who Should Apply? Target Professional Backgrounds

Mercor is actively recruiting across two primary profiles within the broader energetic materials sector, looking for both chemical specialists and operational engineers. Ideal applicants typically emerge from the following backgrounds:

  • Hazardous Device Specialists & Public Safety Professionals: Law enforcement or military explosive ordnance disposal (EOD) experts experienced in render-safe procedures, device assessment, and post-blast investigations.
  • Specialty Materials Chemists: Chemists specializing in pyrotechnics, propellants, or high-energy formulations, with practical experience in synthesis, performance testing, and compound behavior.
  • Federal Certified Materials Specialists & Incident Analysts: Forensic professionals skilled in material identification, structural analysis, and hazardous scene evaluation.
  • Propulsion & Effects Engineers: Aerospace or mechanical engineers with direct design experience in munitions, rocket propulsion systems, and energy transfer.
  • Commercial Blasting Engineers: Industry professionals expert in industrial mining explosives, specialized materials handling, secure storage, and transport compliance.

The Importance of Technical Writing & Communication

Beyond hands-on operational or laboratory expertise, this position is intensely writing-focused. Every evaluation, prompt, and grading decision requires clear, articulated rationales that non-specialist engineers and auditors can easily follow. Candidates with a proven portfolio of technical writing, published research, incident reporting, or expert witness testimony will have a distinct advantage during selection.
