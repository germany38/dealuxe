Why Specialized Clinical Expertise Matters in Frontier AI Development

Historically, artificial intelligence training depended on broad internet data collection. However, when AI models encounter sensitive domains such as adolescent healthcare, behavioral psychology, and pediatric eating disorders, generic data sources are wholly inadequate. Inaccurate clinical guidance or flawed risk evaluations in adolescent psychiatry carry severe real-world consequences.

micro1 builds the human intelligence layer for frontier AI by vetting and engaging top-tier professionals. As an Eating Disorder Specialist on the platform, your real-world clinical judgment, familiarity with diagnostic manuals, and mastery of medical risk protocols are directly translated into high-quality training data, evaluations, and structured feedback loops. No prior software coding experience is necessary; your clinical background is the core asset driving the technology forward.

Detailed Scope of Work and Daily Responsibilities:

  • Case Review and Medical Risk Evaluation: Review and assess complex adolescent eating disorder cases, providing comprehensive written evaluations of clinical and medical risks.
  • Diagnostic Application: Apply DSM-5 criteria and validated screening instruments (such as EDE-Q and SCOFF) to accurately characterize clinical presentations.
  • Medical Stability and Escalation: Determine medical stability and identify patient cases requiring urgent medical escalation using recognized frameworks such as MEED (Medical Emergencies in Eating Disorders).
  • Modality and Intervention Assessment: Evaluate the appropriateness of specific treatment modalities (such as Family-Based Treatment [FBT] or Cognitive Behavioral Therapy-Enhanced [CBT-E]) in relation to the adolescent's specific clinical stage.
  • Comorbidity Analysis: Identify comorbidities and safeguarding concerns (such as co-occurring anxiety, depression, or self-harm) that influence severity or required clinical intervention.
  • Clear Guidance Production: Produce clear, actionable guidance suitable for non-specialists, firmly grounded in current evidence-based clinical standards and guidelines.
  • Peer Benchmarking: Benchmark clinical judgments against peer data and established medical guidelines to ensure maximum consistency, safety, and quality.

💡 Maximizing Project Success: To ensure long-term collaboration and stable task flow on micro1, experts are encouraged to fully complete the structured application and onboarding process, followed by successfully executing at least 10 hours of active project work. Meeting this vital milestone establishes your reliability, ensures smooth onboarding administration, and unlocks ongoing expert assignments.

Preferred Qualifications and Professional Background

Due to the sensitivity and technical depth of adolescent behavioral healthcare, micro1 maintains rigorous vetting standards for this role. Candidates should ideally possess the following credentials:

  • Clinical Experience: 3 to 5+ years of post-qualification experience working directly with adolescent patients in a clinical, psychiatric, or hospital setting.
  • Specialized Background: Specialist background in eating disorders, child/adolescent psychiatry, clinical psychology, or adolescent/paediatric medicine with a primary focus on eating disorders.
  • Active Licensing: An active clinical license or professional registration in good standing, with recent experience managing an adolescent caseload or active practice within the past two years.
  • Diagnostic Proficiency: Fluent command of evidence-based assessment tools (DSM-5, EDE-Q, SCOFF) and medical risk assessment protocols (e.g., MEED).
  • Interventional Knowledge: Familiarity with established interventions such as Family-Based Treatment (FBT) or CBT-E.
  • Communication Standards: Exceptional written English skills (native level, or equivalent to IELTS 7.5+/writing 7.0+, TOEFL 100+, or Cambridge C1–C2). Desirable attributes include formal FBT or CBT-E training and experience in adolescent medical stabilization.
