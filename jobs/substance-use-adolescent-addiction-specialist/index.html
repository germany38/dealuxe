The Intersection of Clinical Practice and Frontier Artificial Intelligence

Healthcare artificial intelligence cannot afford generic approximations. When dealing with adolescent substance use, an AI tool must accurately distinguish between normal adolescent experimentation and clinically significant substance use disorders (SUD). More importantly, the system must recognize acute safety threats, such as overdose risks, severe withdrawal symptoms, and immediate psychiatric crises, where delayed intervention can lead to tragic outcomes.

To bridge this critical safety gap, micro1 builds the human intelligence layer for advanced AI systems. By partnering with clinicians who possess years of hands-on experience treating adolescents, micro1 transforms complex clinical reasoning into high-quality training data, evaluations, and interactive feedback loops. No prior software coding experience is required; your clinical judgment, mastery of diagnostic criteria, and dedication to patient safety are what train the models to reason accurately.

Detailed Scope of Work and Responsibilities:

  • Case Review & Risk Documentation: Review clinical case scenarios describing adolescent substance use and draft comprehensive, structured assessments evaluating risk level, severity, and intervention urgency.
  • Framework Application: Utilize established clinical frameworks, including the DSM-5 Substance Use Disorder (SUD) criteria and American Society of Addiction Medicine (ASAM) criteria, to stage severity and recommend appropriate levels of care.
  • Intervention Evaluation: Assess the safety and clinical appropriateness of proposed and alternative interventions, including pharmacological options (such as MOUD/MAT protocols) when relevant to under-18 populations.
  • Behavioral Differentiation: Distinguish rigorously between developmentally normal adolescent experimentation and clinically significant, maladaptive patterns of substance use.
  • Safeguarding & Escalation: Identify and flag acute withdrawal, overdose, and safeguarding risks that necessitate immediate clinical escalation or emergency response.
  • Actionable Guidance Generation: Develop clear, well-articulated written guidance designed for non-specialists or downstream models, explaining clinical rationales while referencing authoritative standards.
  • Peer Benchmarking: Compare and benchmark your clinical assessments against peer input and published empirical best practices to ensure absolute consistency and rigor.

💡 Maximizing Your Professional Impact: To establish long-term project stability, unlock higher-tier assignments, and ensure seamless compensation, experts accepted into micro1 projects are strongly encouraged to complete the full application onboarding and commit to achieving at least 10 hours of active project work.

Preferred Qualifications and Professional Background

Because micro1 collaborates with top-tier artificial intelligence research labs, vetting standards for clinical projects are rigorous. Ideal candidates for the Substance Use - Adolescent Addiction Specialist position exhibit robust professional credentials:

  • Clinical Experience: 3 to 5+ years of post-certification experience working directly with adolescents impacted by substance use disorders in clinical, inpatient, or outpatient settings.
  • Specialized Expertise: A clinical specialty in addiction medicine, child/adolescent psychiatry, or clinical psychology with a primary professional focus on youth behavioral health and substance use disorders.
  • Active Licensure: An active clinical license or professional registration in good standing, supported by recent (<2 years) adolescent caseload experience.
  • Diagnostic Tool Proficiency: Demonstrated fluency with adolescent substance use screening and severity staging instruments, including CRAFFT, S2BI, DSM-5 criteria, and ASAM criteria.
  • Specialized Protocol Knowledge: Familiarity with medication for opioid use disorder (MOUD/MAT) and withdrawal management guidelines tailored for pediatric and adolescent populations.
  • Communication Standard: Research-standard written English proficiency (native speaker or equivalent certification such as IELTS 7.5+ overall/writing 7.0+, TOEFL 100+, or Cambridge C1–C2). Additional training in motivational interviewing or a background in publishing clinical guidelines is highly advantageous.
