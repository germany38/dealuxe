Inside the Lab: Pioneering Robotic Dexterity

Building artificial intelligence that understands physical manipulation requires more than just algorithms—it requires capturing human fine motor control across thousands of distinct physical interactions. Robots must learn how much pressure to apply when opening a container, how to reorient irregularly shaped objects, and how to adapt to environmental variations seamlessly.

As a Robotics Data Trainer on this project, your primary objective is to act as the physical anchor for advanced machine learning models. Operating directly on-site in a specialized San Francisco laboratory environment, you will capture vital motion and manipulation data that teaches robotic systems how to interact with the physical world with human-like precision.

Core Competencies & Required Profile

Unlike traditional software engineering roles, this position values mechanical empathy, spatial awareness, physical endurance, and absolute precision over prior AI coding experience. Candidates who thrive in this environment typically demonstrate the following traits:

  • Fine Motor Control: Exceptional manual dexterity and precision handling of varied physical objects.
  • Uncompromising Attention to Detail: Meticulous dedication to following complex, step-by-step experimental protocols without deviation.
  • Physical Endurance: Comfort with repetitive, hands-on tasks and the stamina to maintain peak focus throughout extended participation windows.
  • Reliability & Accountability: A pristine track record of punctuality, professionalism, and personal accountability in prior professional, academic, or operational roles.
  • Technical Agility: The ability to quickly learn basic hardware interfaces, adapt to new lab setups, and troubleshoot minor technical hiccups.

Detailed Scope of Work & Daily Operations

Contractors selected for this pioneering initiative are entrusted with vital physical data collection workflows. Day-to-day responsibilities within the San Francisco laboratory include:

  • UMI Gripper Operation: Operating a specialized handheld UMI gripper device onsite to execute repetitive manipulation tasks, ranging from picking up objects and opening containers to performing routine household actions across diverse environments.
  • Protocol Adherence: Closely following detailed, written task instructions and operational protocols with absolute exactness to guarantee clean, consistent data capture for robot learning algorithms.
  • Environment Resetting: Systematically resetting physical workstations and testing environments between task attempts to ensure optimal baseline conditions.
  • Quality Verification: Verifying the integrity and quality of each data submission before progressing to subsequent experimental cycles.
  • Anomaly Detection: Promptly detecting and communicating hardware malfunctions, ambiguous instructions, or compromised recordings to project leadership for rapid resolution.
  • Observation & Feedback: Documenting qualitative observations, reporting operational roadblocks, and suggesting practical process improvements through both written and verbal channels.
