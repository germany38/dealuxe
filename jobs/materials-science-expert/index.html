The Core Mission: Why Frontier AI Needs Computational Materials Experts

Training modern artificial intelligence to understand physical and engineering sciences presents a unique hurdle. While generic text models can synthesize general definitions, they routinely falter when required to execute rigorous scientific calculations, configure atomistic simulations, or diagnose convergence failures in thermodynamic models. An AI model advising an engineer on material selection or microstructure stability cannot afford hallucinations or invalid physical assumptions.

micro1 bridges this gap by gathering elite academic researchers, national laboratory scientists, and industry R&D specialists to create verified, high-fidelity training data. In this role, you are not simply writing text; you are constructing solver-ready simulation inputs, running programmatic parameter sweeps, and evaluating whether AI-generated engineering solutions are computationally valid and physically meaningful.

What You Will Work On: Core Responsibilities

  • Problem Solving & Validation: Solve and validate complex computational materials-science and materials-engineering problems across diverse thermal, mechanical, electrical, and chemical properties.
  • Atomic & Structural Modeling: Construct material structures, atomic configurations, alloy compositions, and solver-ready inputs for advanced simulation engines.
  • Programmatic Automation via Python: Use Python scripts, Jupyter notebooks, and scientific libraries (such as NumPy, SciPy, pandas, and Matplotlib) to generate inputs, automate calculations, conduct parameter sweeps, and process results.
  • Simulation Execution: Run atomistic, electronic-structure, molecular-dynamics, continuum, or electrochemical simulations using industry-standard tools accessible via CLI, scripting interfaces, or configuration files (e.g., LAMMPS, ASE, pymatgen, Quantum ESPRESSO, FEniCSx, CalculiX, Elmer, PyBaMM).
  • Diagnostic Troubleshooting: Diagnose failed calculations, invalid atomic structures, convergence problems, numerical instability, and incorrect physical assumptions.
  • AI Model Auditing: Review AI-generated solutions for scientific correctness, identifying subtle flaws in modeling parameters and developing reproducible reference solutions.

💡 Crucial Technical Requirement: Experience limited exclusively to Graphical User Interfaces (GUIs) will not suffice for this role. Task solutions must be fully reproducible through code, scripts, configuration files, or command-line interfaces (CLI).

Preferred Qualifications and Background

Because micro1 collaborates with top-tier AI labs, vetting standards are exceptionally rigorous. Ideal candidates possess advanced academic credentials and demonstrated practical proficiency in computational science:

  • Educational Background: An MS or PhD in Materials Science and Engineering, Metallurgy, or a closely related discipline; or an MS or PhD in Mechanical Engineering or Chemical Engineering with a substantial materials focus.
  • Domain Mastery: A robust understanding of materials behavior, phase transformations, thermodynamic principles, and structure-property relationships.
  • Scientific Programming Skills: Practical proficiency in Python, coupled with familiarity with scientific tools accessible through scripting or command-line interfaces.
  • Modeling Experience: Demonstrated background in computational materials modeling, atomistic simulation, characterization, or materials informatics gained through academic research, national labs, or industrial R&D.
  • Analytical Rigor: The ability to justify modeling assumptions, approximations, and convergence criteria, while clearly distinguishing computational errors from genuine physical phenomena.
