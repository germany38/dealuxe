Bridging Language and Science: The Evolution of AI Safety and Frontier Model Training

Artificial intelligence has advanced at a breakneck speed, transforming from experimental algorithms into powerhouse systems capable of complex reasoning, scientific analysis, and sophisticated communication. Detailed Job Description & Core Responsibilities

This role is meticulously designed for scientific minds who possess deep domain knowledge in specialized technical disciplines such as chemistry, biology, or related core sciences. While previous experience training artificial intelligence or machine learning models is entirely unnecessary—as comprehensive workflow training is provided—your scientific judgment is paramount.

Your primary objective will be to evaluate how next-generation AI systems interpret, process, and respond to complex technical prompts formulated in the specific variety of Dutch spoken in Belgium. You will act as an intellectual safeguard, ensuring that advanced models handle specialized scientific topics accurately, safely, and responsibly.

Core Responsibilities Include:

  • Expert Prompt Generation: Craft high-level, expert prompts in Belgian Dutch across advanced scientific topics, testing the absolute limits and capabilities of frontier AI algorithms.
  • Response Annotation & Evaluation: Critically assess, annotate, and grade model outputs for scientific accuracy, clarity, nuance, and helpfulness.
  • Safety & Compliance Review: Ensure appropriate handling of sensitive, regulated, or dual-use scientific content, assessing how models manage risk and safety guardrails.
  • Structured Classification: Apply rigorous, structured guidelines to categorize prompts and conversational threads, generating clean datasets for AI development labs.

Candidate Requirements & Academic Qualifications

Because these frontier models are tested against extreme parameters involving complex technical concepts, the prerequisites for this position are specialized and rigorous.

Essential Requirements for Applicants:
  • Academic Credentials: A completed or ongoing PhD in chemistry, biology, or a closely related scientific discipline, providing the depth required to write prompts across chemical, biological, or radiological/nuclear domains.
  • Language Proficiency: Native or near-native fluency in Dutch specifically as it is spoken in Belgium, complemented by business-level written English.
  • Geographic Location: Must be currently based in Belgium. (Note: This specific listing covers the Belgian variety of Dutch; separate opportunities exist for Netherlands Dutch).
  • Technical Familiarity: Deep, practical familiarity with modern laboratory protocols, experimental procedures, and computational techniques within your scientific subfield.
  • Sound Judgment: Demonstrated maturity and sound judgment regarding scientific safety, biosecurity, and the responsible management of dual-use information.

Preferred & Nice-to-Have Backgrounds: Experience covering radiological and nuclear subjects from a chemistry background (such as radiochemistry, nuclear chemistry, energetic materials, or radiation physics); targeted depth in subfields like organic/inorganic synthesis, molecular biology, microbiology, virology, or genetics; coursework touching on chemical safety and security; and prior experience reviewing or red-teaming technical documentation.
