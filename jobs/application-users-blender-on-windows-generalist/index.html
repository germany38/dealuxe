Deep Dive: What Does a Blender Generalist Do for AI Labs?

Artificial intelligence models are rapidly expanding beyond simple text and 2D imagery into complex spatial computing, 3D asset generation, and interactive graphics. However, machine learning models cannot learn intricate topology, sculpting techniques, or rigging procedures without expert human guidance. That is where experienced practitioners step in.

As a Blender Generalist at Mercor, your core responsibility is to apply your practical knowledge of 3D modeling, sculpting, animation, and rendering to evaluate, train, and refine AI capabilities. You are bridging the gap between professional creative workflows and cutting-edge machine learning innovation.

Core Focus Areas in 3D & Post-Production:

  • Open-Source 3D Modeling: Utilizing Blender's vast toolset for mesh creation, modifier stacks, UV unwrapping, and material shading.
  • Sculpting & Texturing: Applying high-detail digital sculpting workflows and texture painting principles that AI systems need to comprehend.
  • Animation & Rendering: Setting up keyframes, armature rigging, camera paths, and rendering engines (Cycles/Eevee) to demonstrate proper pipeline execution.
  • Technical Problem Solving: Providing expert evaluations and structured data on how complex 3D tasks are structured and solved within Windows environments.

Crucial Technology Requirements: Monitors and Resolution

Because the workflows involve detailed visual evaluation, UI navigation, and screen capture, candidates must meet specific display standards. You must be able to capture screenshots with more than 2.5 megapixels, which can be satisfied via one of two options:

Option A — Single High-Resolution Monitor

Any display meeting or exceeding these standard aspect ratios and resolutions:

  • 16:9 Aspect Ratio: 2560×1440 (QHD), 3840×2160 (4K), or 5120×2880 (5K).
  • 16:10 Aspect Ratio: 2240×1400, 2560×1600, or 2880×1800.
  • 21:9 Ultrawide: 2560×1080, 3440×1440, or 3840×1600.
  • 3:2 Aspect Ratio: 2160×1440, 2256×1504, 2736×1824, or 3000×2000.
  • 4:3 Aspect Ratio: 2048×1536.

Option B — Dual-Monitor Setup

Two displays positioned side by side and captured as a single spanning image. Two 1920×1080 (or higher) monitors qualify (note: a single 1080p screen out of that pair does not qualify on its own).
