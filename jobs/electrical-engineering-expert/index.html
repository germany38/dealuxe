Comprehensive Role Overview & Core Objectives

Modern industrial design and utility grid management operate under extreme safety stakes and stringent regulatory frameworks. AI systems deployed in these sectors cannot afford textbook approximations; they must mirror the rigorous, multi-stakeholder reasoning of senior engineering leaders. Contributors in this role are tasked with building sophisticated evaluation benchmarks and training tasks calibrated to the exact complexity and production scale found within Fortune 500 organizations and major technology manufacturers.

Whether your expertise lies within domestic standards or international frameworks, the engagement accommodates two dedicated standards tracks:

  • International Track: Focused heavily on International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC) standards and global manufacturing workflows.
  • US Track: Centered around National Electrical Code (NEC / NFPA 70), National Electrical Safety Code (NESC), and IEEE standards.

Qualified professionals skilled in either or both tracks are encouraged to apply, designing complex enterprise engineering scenarios, drafting pristine reference outputs, and authoring precise evaluation rubrics.

Key Responsibilities & Scope of Work

Working as a contractor on Mercor, your day-to-day focus will center on creating rigorous testing environments and evaluation datasets that challenge and refine frontier AI systems. Your responsibilities include:

Detailed Task Breakdown:

  • Scenario Construction: Construct complex enterprise electrical engineering scenarios spanning multi-stakeholder design reviews, intricate circuit and systems design cycles, and large-scale manufacturing or regulatory certification processes.
  • Cross-Domain Power & Grid Coverage: Build rigorous tasks across power systems and grid infrastructure, semiconductor and PCB design, embedded systems, firmware architecture, advanced signal processing, and sophisticated control systems engineering.
  • Tool-Driven Methodologies: Develop engineering tasks that incorporate professional enterprise tooling such as Altium Designer, Cadence, MATLAB/Simulink, SPICE simulation platforms, and enterprise PLM/EDA systems.
  • Regulatory & Methodological Rigor: Apply advanced methodologies—including circuit analysis and simulation, EMC/EMI compliance testing, control theory, reliability analysis, and failure mode and effects analysis (FMEA)—against recognized US or international standards tracks.
  • Executive Technical Narratives: Produce comprehensive reference design specifications, in-depth engineering analyses, and executive-level technical narratives that reflect true industrial problem-solving.
  • Rubric Authoring: Author sophisticated grading rubrics that effectively distinguish authentic enterprise electrical engineering judgment from generic, textbook-level or coursework recall.

Ideal Qualifications & Professional Background

Because these tasks directly train models intended for mission-critical enterprise environments, the selection criteria prioritize deep, hands-on industrial leadership over theoretical knowledge alone.

Key Professional Requirements:
  • Industry Tenure: 5+ years of hands-on experience working as an electrical engineer, principal designer, or engineering lead at a Fortune 500 or major technology, industrial, or energy corporation (e.g., Intel, Texas Instruments, General Electric, Siemens, Tesla, ABB, Schneider Electric).
  • Direct Ownership: Proven track record of direct ownership over large-scale circuit designs, critical power systems, or enterprise engineering certification programs.
  • Tool & Standard Mastery: Complete fluency in enterprise electrical engineering tools and methodologies, coupled with an intimate understanding of how safety regulations, compliance standards (NEC / NFPA 70, IEEE, UL, FCC, IEC), and cross-functional design governance function in practice.
  • Credentials & Authorship: Holding a professional engineering credential (such as a US PE or international equivalents like CEng or EUR ING) alongside prior experience writing technical rubrics, engineering training curricula, or enterprise design documentation serves as a significant advantage.
