The Mission: Why AI Labs Need Frontline Practitioners

Artificial intelligence models are only as robust as the data and safety guidelines used to train them. When dealing with sensitive technical fields—such as explosive materials, post-blast residues, hazardous industrial compounds, or chemical storage regulations—generalist content moderators simply do not possess the granular knowledge required to draw accurate safety lines.

This domain lives squarely on the dual-use line more than almost any other sector in science and technology. Consider the nature of forensic casework: the exact same residue analysis methodology that closes an investigative case also describes what materials were utilized. The same diversion indicators used by regulatory inspectors to catch non-compliant licensees detail how vulnerabilities might be exploited. A model that blindly refuses a routine, legitimate forensic identification question fails its core utility. Conversely, a model that answers an unsafe, adversarial prompt poses significant risks. Drawing that razor-thin line reliably is why Mercor relies on seasoned practitioners rather than general writers.

Core Responsibilities of the Role:

  • Prompt Generation: Write challenging, single-turn prompts within your specific professional domain, systematically labeled across three complexity tiers: benign, dual-use, and adversarial.
  • Response Evaluation: Rigorously review the AI model's generated outputs against defined policy standards to judge whether the model handled the scenario accurately and safely.
  • Reference Answer Authoring: Compose comprehensive reference answers detailing what a correct, safe response looks like, alongside the underlying technical reasoning.

💡 Note on Compliance: All work performed through Mercor for this project strictly avoids classified, restricted, or export-controlled data, as well as any material bound by active NDAs or prepublication review obligations. Practitioners with active or past clearances can participate safely by working strictly within unclassified frameworks.

Ideal Professional Background and Qualifications

Mercor is searching for individuals who have spent years working active casework, field investigations, or regulatory oversight under recognized national certifications. General academic interest is not sufficient; this role demands frontline domain expertise.

Ideal candidates typically possess experience in backgrounds such as:

  • Federally Certified Explosives Specialists or national equivalents focusing on scene reconstruction and materials analysis.
  • Forensic Laboratory Scientists specializing in the identification of energetic materials, chemical compounds, and post-blast residues.
  • Post-Incident Analysts and Investigators reporting for specialized agencies, public safety bodies, or private forensic laboratories.
  • Regulatory Inspection Professionals managing licensees and permittees—focusing on safe storage, chain-of-custody records, and diversion indicators.
  • Expert Witnesses or Caseworkers offering specialized testimony and analytical reports on energetic or hazardous materials.

Beyond Technical Depth: The Importance of Technical Writing

While deep technical mastery is mandatory, this is fundamentally a writing-intensive endeavor. Every single evaluation, classification, and judgment you submit must include a clear, professional written rationale that a non-specialist program manager can easily follow. Candidates with a proven history of technical writing, published research papers, industry whitepapers, or formal expert witness testimony will find their profiles heavily favored.
