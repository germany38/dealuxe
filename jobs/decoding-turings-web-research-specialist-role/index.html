About Turing and the Mission Behind AI Evaluation

Turing helps top-tier technology enterprises and AI labs in two critical ways: collaborating with leading labs to advance frontier model capabilities in reasoning, multimodality, and STEM knowledge, and leveraging those breakthroughs to build mission-critical real-world AI systems.

The Web Research Specialist role sits directly at the center of this mission. Rather than traditional content writing or standard subject matter expert tasks, this engagement focuses entirely on investigative research and red-teaming.

What the Role Entails & Daily Workflow

When working as a Web Research Specialist, your core objective is to build evaluation benchmarks for frontier AI browsing agents. Specifically, your job is to design research problems that a state-of-the-art AI cannot solve—even when granted full web access and multiple attempts.

Instead of searching for answers to a question, you will work backward:

  • Start with a Verifiable Fact: Begin with an authentic, verified data point or event.
  • Construct Hard-to-Locate Questions: Formulate natural-language research questions that make discovering that fact extremely difficult through standard search queries.
  • Develop Independent Clues: Craft checkable clues spanning multiple fact types—including dates, people, places, organizations, works, events, records, and quantities—governed by precise constraints.
  • Audit Validation Trails: Document every search query run, the results obtained, and provide precise citations to exact pages, tables, and sections (rather than generic homepages).

You Are a Perfect Fit If You Possess:

  • Educational or Professional Background: A Master’s degree OR 3+ years of relevant professional experience.
  • Advanced Open-Web Research Skills: Proven ability to navigate primary records, government databases, archives, registries, and complex PDF documents.
  • Specialized Domain Experience: Backgrounds in reference librarianship, archival research, investigative journalism, professional fact-checking, OSINT, due diligence, KYC, patent/legal discovery, genealogy, or competitive puzzle-hunt construction.
  • Documentation Rigor: Native or near-native written English with a high tolerance for structured documentation—the evidence trail forms the majority of the work.
