The Core Mission: Why AI Labs Need Chemistry Domain Experts

As large language models become increasingly sophisticated, they process vast repositories of technical literature. While this grants them incredible academic capabilities, it also presents distinct safety challenges. Frontier AI labs must ensure that models do not inadvertently assist with dangerous, illicit, or uncontrolled chemical synthesis, while simultaneously ensuring they do not overreact and refuse benign, educational, or industrial safety queries.

Generalist reviewers lack the granular understanding required to evaluate chemical hazards accurately. In chemistry, the safety line often sits precisely between reaction mechanism and procedural execution—a distinction that only a practitioner who has physically run reactions can discern.

What Your Daily Task Workflow Looks Like:

  • Prompt Generation: Write challenging, single-turn prompts within your specialized domain, meticulously categorized across three distinct levels: benign, dual-use, and adversarial.
  • Model Response Evaluation: Critically analyze model outputs against defined institutional safety policies, judging whether the model handled the nuance correctly.
  • Authoring Reference Answers: Draft comprehensive technical gold-standard responses demonstrating what a correct model output looks like, accompanied by rigorous scientific reasoning for your judgment.

💡 The Calibration Challenge: The hardest part of this work is not raw difficulty—it is calibration. A benign prompt that a model wrongly refuses is just as problematic as an adversarial prompt it incorrectly answers. Your expert judgment ensures the balance is struck correctly.
