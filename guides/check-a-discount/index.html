Is that discount actually a saving?

Published 21 November 2026 · Dealux · Method guide, not a product test

A “40% off” label compares a sale price with a reference price chosen by the seller. It does not tell you whether another retailer charges less, whether the product usually sells at that price, or whether you need it. Use the following checks before treating a promotion as a saving.

1. Match the exact product

Compare the manufacturer model number, size, storage capacity, material, included accessories, condition, and pack quantity. Two laptops with the same family name can have different screens and memory. A refurbished item is not a like-for-like comparison with a new one. If the model number is missing, ask the seller before paying.

2. Choose a useful comparison price

Look for the current delivered price at two other sellers. If a price-history service covers the exact model, check its usual price over several weeks, not just its highest recorded price. Without a reliable history, describe the offer as cheaper than a specific alternative today; do not assume the crossed-out price represents a normal selling price.

3. Calculate the delivered total

Apply a valid coupon to the eligible amount, then add shipping, tax, and mandatory fees. Do not count conditional cashback as money already saved: it may depend on tracking, approval, or keeping the purchase beyond the return period.

4. Check whether waiting costs less

A timer tells you when a promotion may end; it does not establish value. If your current item works and the price history is unclear, save the model number and revisit it later. If you need a replacement immediately, prioritize availability, delivery reliability, and the return policy over a theoretical future discount.

Before checkout

  • Capture the final total and coupon conditions.
  • Confirm who actually sells and ships the item.
  • Check whether membership or automatic renewal is required.
  • Read exclusions for clearance, opened, and final-sale items.
