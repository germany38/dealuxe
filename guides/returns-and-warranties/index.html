Check the exit before you buy

Published 21 November 2026 · Dealux · General shopping guidance

A purchase can fit your budget and still be expensive to undo. Before buying, separate the seller’s voluntary return policy from the manufacturer’s warranty and any statutory consumer rights that apply where you live. Each solves a different problem.

Returns: what if you change your mind?

Find when the return window starts: order date, dispatch, or delivery. Check whether you must request authorization or have the parcel arrive back within that window. Look for exclusions covering opened electronics, hygiene products, customized goods, digital downloads, and final-sale stock.

For a bulky purchase, ask for a return freight estimate in writing. A “returns accepted” label does not mean the seller pays collection charges or refunds original delivery fees. Keep the packaging until you have checked the item and understand the conditions.

Work out the downside

Suppose a chair costs $240, delivery costs $35, return freight is $60, and the policy applies a 15% restocking fee to the item price. If original delivery is non-refundable and those conditions apply, returning it costs $131: $35 + $60 + $36. This is an illustrative calculation; defective-item rights may override a seller’s ordinary change-of-mind policy.

Warranties: what if it stops working?

Check who provides service, how long coverage lasts, and whether parts, labor, and shipping are included. A manufacturer may restrict coverage to purchases from authorized sellers or to a particular country. Marketplace fulfillment alone does not establish that a seller is authorized. Ask the manufacturer if coverage matters to your decision.

Marketplace and cross-border purchases

Record the seller’s legal or trading name and location. The platform, payment provider, and seller can have separate dispute deadlines. Import duties and international return costs may erase a price advantage. Do not assume that a platform’s familiar name means every listing has the same protections.

Save a small evidence pack

  1. Save the listing, seller identity, and promised delivery date.
  2. Keep the receipt and the policy that applied at checkout.
  3. Inspect the delivery promptly and photograph damage, including packaging.
  4. Contact the seller through a traceable channel before relevant deadlines.

This guide is not legal advice. Consumer protections vary by location and purchase type. Read the FTC’s warranty guidance for US purchases or consult your local consumer authority. Next: check whether the discount is real.
