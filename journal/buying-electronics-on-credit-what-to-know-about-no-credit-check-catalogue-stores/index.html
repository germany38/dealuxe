Buying Electronics on Credit: What to Know About No-Credit-Check Catalogue Stores

Disclosure: Dealux may earn a commission on purchases made through links on this page, at no extra cost to you. This is general information, not financial advice. Credit is subject to terms and status; check the official site and read the agreement before you buy.

Spreading the cost of a phone, laptop or appliance can make essentials affordable. Catalogue stores that offer credit without traditional credit checks appeal to many shoppers. Trusty describes itself as a no-credit-check catalogue store and mobile phone provider offering credit on over 10,000 items, with 0% APR and no fees, based as a physical store in the West Midlands as well as online. Credit is a serious commitment, so it pays to read the terms.

How catalogue credit works

You choose items, agree to pay in instalments and receive the goods. The total cost, number of payments and consequences of missed payments are set out in the agreement. Even with 0% APR, the overall price may be higher than a cash price elsewhere, so compare.

Questions to ask before you buy

  1. What is the total amount payable?
  2. How long is the repayment period and how much is each payment?
  3. What happens if I miss a payment?
  4. Can I repay early, and are there fees?
  5. How is the product priced compared with other retailers?
  6. What are the returns and warranty terms?

Budget first

Add the monthly payment to your outgoings and check that it is affordable. Credit you cannot repay can create problems.

Alternatives

Consider saving, second-hand or refurbished options, or low-cost lenders. If you are struggling with debt, free advice services can help.

A simple affordability check

Before signing any agreement, write down your monthly income and essential costs, then see how much is truly spare. A payment that fits only if nothing else goes wrong is too risky. Remember that phones, laptops and appliances may need repairs or replacement, so consider the warranty and what happens if the product fails during the repayment period.

Comparing the total cost

Add up all the payments across the whole term and compare it with the cash price from other retailers, including refurbished or sale options. Sometimes a retailer that offers credit charges a higher product price, so a 0% APR offer may not be the cheapest route overall.

Where to get help

If you are struggling with existing debt, free and impartial advice services can help you plan and negotiate with creditors. Seek help early, before missed payments make things harder.

Common mistakes

Focusing on the monthly price only. Not reading the agreement. Taking more credit than you need.

FAQ

Does no credit check mean no assessment? Affordability checks may still apply. Is 0% APR always cheaper? Compare the total cost.

Conclusion

Credit can help, but only when it is affordable and clear. If you want to see what Trusty offers, review the terms carefully on its site.
