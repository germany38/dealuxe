Remortgaging in the UK: How to Compare Deals and Understand Cashback Offers

When to start looking

Many lenders allow you to secure a new deal several months before your current one ends. Starting early gives you time to compare, gather documents and avoid slipping onto a more expensive rate. Check your current lender's early repayment charges, because leaving before the end of a deal can be costly.

What to compare

  1. Interest rate: fixed rates give certainty, while variable and tracker rates can move.
  2. Total cost: include arrangement fees, valuation costs and legal fees, not just the headline rate.
  3. Deal length: longer fixes offer stability but may carry bigger exit charges.
  4. Early repayment charges: check how much you would pay if you left early.
  5. Overpayment allowance: see how much extra you can pay without penalty.
  6. Product features: offset accounts or payment holidays may matter to you.

Understanding cashback

Cashback is a payment made on completion of a remortgage. It can feel like a bonus, but it is only valuable if the overall deal is competitive. A slightly higher interest rate over several years can cost more than the cashback you receive. Always compare the total cost over the deal period with and without the cashback. Read the eligibility rules, because cashback is usually tied to specific completions and conditions.

The role of a broker

A broker can search many lenders and help with applications. Some charge fees, some are paid by lenders, and some do both. Ask clearly how the broker is paid, whether the fee is refundable and how many lenders they compare. A service that states there are no broker fees should still explain how it is funded, which you can ask about before you proceed.

Get your documents ready

Lenders usually ask for proof of income, ID, bank statements, details of your current mortgage and information about your property. Have them ready to speed up the process.

Check affordability

Think about your budget if rates rise. Use the lender's calculators, but also run your own numbers. Consider your job security, other debts and plans such as having children or moving.

Watch the timeline

Remortgages can take several weeks, depending on valuations and legal work. Keep track of key dates, such as when your current deal ends and when your offer expires.

Common mistakes

Letting the deal lapse. Choosing on cashback alone. Ignoring fees. Not checking early repayment charges. Not asking how the broker is paid.

FAQ

Is it worth remortgaging? It depends on your current rate, your equity and the costs. Can I borrow more? Some people release equity, but this increases debt, so think carefully. Will my credit score matter? Lenders will check your circumstances.

Conclusion

A good remortgage starts with early comparison and a clear view of the total cost. Treat cashback as a bonus, not the main reason to choose a deal. To see how the service works, review Cashback Remortgages and check the terms carefully.
