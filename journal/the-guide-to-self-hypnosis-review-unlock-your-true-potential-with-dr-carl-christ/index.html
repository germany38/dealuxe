Debunking the Myths: What Is Self-Hypnosis Really?

For decades, pop culture, stage magicians, and Hollywood movies have painted a wildly inaccurate picture of hypnosis. Many people still associate it with losing control, being forced to cluck like a chicken, or falling into a mysterious, zombie-like unconscious trance.

In reality, clinical and practical self-hypnosis has nothing to do with mind control or stage performance. As Dr. Carl Christman explains, self-hypnosis is a completely natural, learnable mental skill. It is a state of focused attention, deep physical relaxation, and heightened receptivity to positive suggestion. You are never asleep, and you never lose control. Instead, you enter a calm, focused mental state where you can intentionally work with your thoughts, replace unhelpful thought patterns, and reinforce your deepest goals.

Think of it as learning how to consciously steer your internal dialogue rather than letting your subconscious run on autopilot. When practiced correctly, self-hypnosis allows you to bypass everyday mental friction and access the deeper reservoirs of your mind.
