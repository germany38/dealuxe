Shortcut Chat: Compare Multiple AI Answers Side by Side

Disclosure: Dealux may earn a commission on purchases made through links on this page, at no extra cost to you.

Choosing an AI chatbot often comes down to guesswork. You pick one, ask your question, and hope the answer is useful. Shortcut Chat takes a different approach. It is an AI chat app where a single prompt goes out to several AI models at once, and their replies appear side by side so you can read and compare them in one place.

How the side-by-side format works

Instead of switching between separate apps and copying text from one window to another, you type your request once. The brand describes access to a wide range of models, including Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, open-source models, and image generation, with many more on top of those. Seeing the replies together makes it easier to notice differences in tone, structure, level of detail, and accuracy.

Who it tends to suit

The format is most useful for people who regularly compare outputs rather than trusting a single source. Common examples include:

  • Writers and editors who want to test different versions of a headline, introduction, or product description.
  • Students and teachers who want to see how several models explain the same concept.
  • Small business owners and marketers testing messaging ideas before they commit to one.
  • Photographers, designers, and creators who want to explore image generation prompts alongside text tools.
  • Trainers and recruiters drafting course outlines, job descriptions, or interview questions.

What to check before you sign up

Before committing, look at the official Shortcut Chat site and confirm the details that matter most to you. Pricing, plan levels, usage limits, and which models are included in each plan can change, so always rely on the current information the brand publishes. Also review its privacy policy and terms, especially if you plan to paste in client files, unpublished work, or personal data. If image generation is important to your workflow, check how it is handled on your plan rather than assuming it is included.

Getting more useful results from several models

Side-by-side comparison works best when your prompt is specific. Give the context, the audience, the length you need, and the format you want. Then look for points where the models agree and where they disagree. Agreement does not guarantee accuracy, and disagreement is a signal to dig deeper. For facts, figures, names, or dates, check the answer against a primary source before you use it. Treat the outputs as drafts and starting points rather than finished work.
