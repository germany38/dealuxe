Pocket Wi-Fi or eSIM? Choosing Connectivity for Business Travel

Business travellers need dependable internet for email, video calls and shared documents. Two main options are pocket Wi-Fi devices and eSIMs. Global WiFi describes itself as backed by Japan's number one Wi-Fi provider for more than nine years, offering premium 5G in over 170 destinations, WPA3 security, award-winning 24/7 support and SAP Concur integration.

How each option works

A pocket Wi-Fi is a small device that creates a Wi-Fi network from a mobile connection. Several devices can connect to it. An eSIM adds mobile data directly to your phone or compatible laptop. Each has strengths and trade-offs.

Pros and cons of pocket Wi-Fi

  • Shared connection: useful for teams or multiple devices.
  • Separate from your phone: keeps your phone battery for calls.
  • Extra device: needs charging and carrying.
  • Delivery and return: you may need to arrange pickup or return.

Pros and cons of eSIM

  • No extra device: installed on your phone.
  • Instant setup: can be done before you travel.
  • Phone battery: hotspot use drains it faster.
  • Compatibility: requires an eSIM-capable device.

Security considerations

Security matters when you handle company data. Look for strong encryption standards such as WPA3 on Wi-Fi devices, use a VPN according to your company policy and avoid public Wi-Fi for confidential work. Keep devices updated and use multi-factor authentication.

Expense and reporting

Frequent travellers often need to record and claim expenses. Integration with expense tools such as SAP Concur can reduce manual work, so check which options your company uses and whether receipts can be exported easily. Ask your finance team about policy and approved providers.

Support when you need it

If a connection fails before a meeting, fast help matters. Check support hours, languages and contact methods. Round-the-clock support is valuable for travellers across time zones.

Think about your team

For groups, one pocket Wi-Fi can connect several laptops, but a single point of failure can be risky. Individual eSIMs reduce that risk. Some teams carry both for backup.

Check coverage and speed

Confirm that your destinations are covered, especially if you travel to less common places. Look at whether 5G is available where you will be and whether speeds are suitable for video calls.

Plan your logistics

Order in advance, check delivery or installation steps and test the connection before leaving. For devices, pack chargers and a spare cable.

Common mistakes

Leaving it to the last minute. Ignoring security policy. Forgetting charging. Not checking return deadlines for rented devices.

FAQ

Is pocket Wi-Fi better for groups? It can be, but individual eSIMs add resilience. Is eSIM cheaper? It depends on the plan and trip length. Do I need a VPN? Follow your company's policy.

Conclusion

The best choice depends on your team, devices and security needs. Compare options, check support and plan ahead. To explore connectivity for business travel, see Global WiFi.
