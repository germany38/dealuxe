The Leveraged Years: A Practical Look at AI Training

Disclosure: Dealux may earn a commission on purchases made through links on this page, at no extra cost to you.

Many professionals know AI tools are changing their field but aren't sure where to begin. The Leveraged Years is an online training brand built around that gap, offering practical AI training for working professionals along with a $49 membership. Before you commit time or money, it helps to know what to look for, so here is a neutral guide to deciding whether it fits your needs. You can review the current offerings on the official website to see the details for yourself.

Who the training is designed for

The brand names attorneys, physicians, CPAs, and project managers as its audience. That range is useful to notice, because AI applications look very different across professions. A lawyer reviewing contracts has different needs than a project manager coordinating a team or an accountant preparing client reports. When you read the program descriptions, check whether the examples reflect tasks you handle in a normal week or whether they feel generic.

What the $49 membership means for your budget

The membership is priced at $49, and the brand describes its prices as approachable. A lower entry cost makes it easier to try training without a large commitment, but the price alone doesn't tell you what you get. Look on the site for what the membership includes, how often materials are updated, how access works, and whether it renews automatically. Add up the cost over several months so you can compare it fairly with other options.

How to compare AI training options

Whether you're looking at this brand or another provider, a few questions will help you sort the options quickly:

  • Relevance: Does the training address your profession or industry specifically?
  • Hands-on practice: Will you work with tools and real tasks, or mostly watch videos?
  • Currency: AI tools change quickly, so ask when the materials were last updated.
  • Time commitment: Can you realistically fit the lessons into your schedule?
  • Total cost: Consider renewal fees and any extras, not just the headline price.

Checking these points against the brand's own pages will give you a clearer picture than any general summary.

Set a goal before you sign up

Training works best when you know what you want to change. Decide on one or two tasks you'd like to handle more efficiently, such as drafting routine documents, summarizing research, or organizing project updates. Then look for lessons that address those tasks directly. Treat the training as a learning tool rather than professional advice, and always check AI-generated output against your own expertise and the rules that apply in your jurisdiction or practice area.

If the training sounds like a good match for your profession and budget, take a few minutes to visit The Leveraged Years and read through the membership details before you decide. Compare what you find with your goals, and choose the option that fits your work best.
