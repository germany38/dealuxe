Many people keep meaning to write a will and never quite get round to it. Thinking about what happens to your home, savings and belongings is not an easy task. Crystal Life Wills offers a will writing service for people who want a will in place without working through every detail alone. This guide explains what the service covers and what to weigh up before you sign up.

Who a professional will writing service suits

You can write a will yourself, but it becomes complicated once family circumstances, property or several different assets are involved. A professional service can take some of that load off. It may suit people with a spouse or partner, children, property, or a mix of savings and possessions, or anyone who would rather have the wording prepared by someone who does this work regularly. If your affairs are very simple, you may be comfortable with a more basic approach, though it is still worth checking the rules that apply to your situation.

What the service covers

According to the provider's description, the service is designed for putting a will in place for yourself, your spouse, your children and/or your assets. That range matters. A will that only reflects one person's wishes may leave gaps when a family has several members or a combination of property, savings and personal items. Check the website for the exact scope of each option, and make sure the will reflects the people and assets you have today rather than a situation from years ago.

What to check on the website before you order

Before committing to any will writing service, read the provider's information carefully. Look for clear details on what is included, how the process runs from first contact to the signed document, and how your personal information is handled. You can visit the Crystal Life Wills website to read the service information directly and compare it with other providers.

Useful points to note include:

  • What is included in the service and whether anything is charged separately
  • Whether the steps are explained clearly before you pay
  • How your personal and financial details are handled
  • How to get in touch if you have questions after the draft is prepared

How to prepare before you start

A well-prepared conversation saves time and helps you get a more useful result. List your assets, such as property, savings, investments and valuable items, and note who you would like to benefit from each one. If you have children under 18, think about who you would want to act as their guardians. It is also worth deciding who you would like to act as your executor. Remember that some assets, such as certain pensions and policies, may pass outside your will and follow their own rules, so check how they are set up. Review your list after major life events such as marriage, divorce, the arrival of a child or a move abroad.

Weighing up whether it is right for you

A professional will is not the only route, and it is not automatically the right choice for everyone. Compare the cost, the level of support and the speed of the process with other options, including solicitors and other will writing services. Ask yourself whether you want one-to-one guidance or are happy working through a clear, structured process. Whichever you choose, a will is a legal document, so check every detail carefully and store the signed original somewhere safe. This article is general information, not legal or financial advice. If your circumstances are complex, speak to a qualified solicitor.

If you have been putting off your will, a professional service may be a practical place to start. Take your time reviewing the details, gather your information, and then visit Crystal Life Wills to see whether its quick, expert approach fits what you need.
