Name-Your-Price Rentals: How Travellers and Hosts Can Make Reverse-Booking Platforms Work

Most rental platforms work the same way: hosts list a property and travellers search. A few reverse the process. Travellers post what they are willing to pay, and hosts contact them if they can offer a match. Plan B Rentals describes a model in which travellers post what they will pay and hosts pay $99 a month to contact them. This guide explains how this type of platform works, and how both sides can use it sensibly.

How reverse booking works

A traveller describes the trip: location, dates, number of guests and a budget. Hosts browse these requests and reach out if they have a suitable property. The traveller then compares the responses and chooses. It can be useful for flexible travellers, off-peak dates and hosts with empty nights.

Tips for travellers

  1. Be specific: include dates, location, guest numbers, pets and any must-have features.
  2. Set a realistic budget: research typical prices for the area and season.
  3. Stay flexible on dates or neighbourhoods if you can, since this attracts more offers.
  4. Compare responses carefully, including cleaning fees, deposits and house rules.
  5. Ask questions before agreeing: check bedding, parking, Wi-Fi and cancellation policy.
  6. Keep communication in writing so details are clear.

Tips for hosts

Because hosts pay a monthly fee to contact travellers, treat it like a lead-generation cost. Track how many travellers you contact, how many respond and how many book. Calculate your cost per booking and compare it with other channels. Contact only requests that fit your property, respond quickly and write a personal message rather than a generic one.

Evaluate the economics

A monthly fee makes sense if it produces enough bookings to cover the cost and leave a profit. For a property that is often empty, a few extra nights can pay for the subscription. For a property that is already well booked, it may not be worth it. Review your numbers after a trial period.

Keep payments safe

Use secure, traceable payment methods and be cautious about requests to pay outside the platform or by unusual methods. Check how payments, deposits and refunds are handled, and keep records of all agreements.

Check the property and the people

Travellers should check photos, reviews, the exact address and the host's identity where possible. Hosts should ask about the purpose of the trip, the guest count and any special requirements, and confirm house rules before check-in.

Understand legal and tax rules

Short-term rentals are regulated in many places. Hosts should check local registration, safety, tax and insurance requirements. Travellers should ensure the rental complies with local rules, because unlicensed rentals can cause problems.

Insurance and safety

Check that you have appropriate insurance. Travellers may want travel insurance, and hosts should review coverage for guests and property. Know emergency contacts and safety features such as smoke alarms.

Common mistakes

Posting a vague request. Setting an unrealistic budget. Paying outside the platform. Hosts ignoring subscription costs. Skipping house rules.

FAQ

Is it cheaper for travellers? It can be if you are flexible, but compare all fees. Do hosts guarantee bookings? No, a fee only provides access to requests. Can I cancel the subscription? Check the terms.

Conclusion
