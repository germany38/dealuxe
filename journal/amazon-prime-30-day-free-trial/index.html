Amazon Prime 30-Day Free Trial: What You Get and How to Start

Visit Amazon →Shop for Amazon →Get the Deals PDF →

Ready to start? Start your Amazon Prime 30-day free trial here

Amazon Prime is one of the most widely used memberships in the world, and the 30-day free trial is the easiest way to see whether it fits your life. You get free delivery, award-winning TV, exclusive deals and more, with no charge during the trial. This guide covers what is included, what it costs afterward, and how to make the most of the month.

What Is the Free Trial?

The Prime free trial gives eligible new members 30 days of full Prime benefits at no cost. After the trial, the membership renews at $14.99 per month plus tax unless you cancel. You can cancel at any time, and if you cancel before the trial ends, you should not be charged for the membership.

Pricing and trial eligibility are set by Amazon, so confirm both on the offer page before you start.

What You Get During the Trial

  • Free delivery. Millions of eligible items ship free, many with fast delivery options. Same-day and next-day delivery are available on select items in some areas.
  • Prime Video. Stream a large library of movies and series, including Amazon Originals.
  • Prime Music. Listen to music and playlists without ads on selected content.
  • Prime Gaming. Claim free games and in-game items on supported platforms.
  • Exclusive deals. Members get early access to promotions, including during big sales events.
  • Reading perks. Prime Reading offers a rotating selection of ebooks, magazines and more.
  • Photo storage. Prime includes unlimited photo storage with Amazon Photos, plus an allowance for video.

Benefits can vary by country and change over time, so check the offer page for the current list.

Why a Free Trial Is a Smart Way to Decide

Most people can judge the value of Prime in a few weeks. During your trial you can place the orders you were already planning, watch a show or two, and see how it feels. If you find you use it often, keeping it will likely feel like a good deal. If not, you can cancel and owe nothing for the trial period.

Is Prime Worth $14.99 a Month?

It depends on how you shop and what you watch. A few quick tests:

  • Do you order online more than a couple of times a month? Free shipping alone can offset the price.
  • Do you already pay for a streaming service? Prime Video may replace or supplement it.
  • Do you buy household basics? Faster delivery and member deals can reduce trips to the store.
  • Do you want one account for shopping, video, music and reading? Prime bundles them.

If you answer yes to two or more, the membership may well pay for itself.

How to Sign Up

  1. Open the offer page using the link below.
  2. Sign in to your Amazon account or create one.
  3. Confirm you are eligible and review the terms.
  4. Add a payment method. You will not be charged during the trial, but you will need one on file.
  5. Start the trial and begin exploring.

Tips for Using Your 30 Days Well

Place your normal orders. Test delivery times on things you would buy anyway.

Try Prime Video. Add a few titles to your watchlist and see whether the library suits you.

Explore the extras. Gaming, music and photo storage are easy to overlook.

Set a reminder. Put a note in your calendar a few days before the trial ends so that you decide on purpose, not by default.

Check your cancellation page. Know where to end the membership before you start, so it is quick if you need it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will I be charged during the trial? No charge is advertised for the 30-day period. After that, the regular price applies unless you cancel.

Can I cancel anytime? Yes. You can cancel from your account settings.

Do I need a credit card? Amazon typically requires a payment method to start. Check the offer page for what it accepts.

Can I try Prime more than once? Trials are generally for new or returning members who meet Amazon's eligibility rules.

Are there cheaper plans? Amazon offers discounted memberships for some groups, including young adults, students and eligible government assistance recipients.
