What the app is built to do

The name points to the core purpose: signing PDF and DOCX documents. Those two formats cover a lot of everyday paperwork, from rental agreements to school forms and freelance contracts, so an app focused on them is easier to judge against the files you actually handle. A practical first step is to open a document you already have and see whether the process feels clear from start to finish.

Who it may suit

A phone-based signing tool tends to make the most sense for people who sign documents now and then and would rather not hunt for a scanner or printer. Tenants, students, freelancers, and anyone who receives forms by email may find it convenient. If you handle a heavy flow of documents across several people every day, compare it with tools designed for team workflows and check the official app page to confirm it covers your needs.

What to compare before you download

  • Pricing: check whether the app is free, paid, or subscription-based, and what each option includes on the official listing.
  • File support: confirm that your PDFs and Word files open and sign correctly, and note any size limits.
  • Privacy: read the developer's privacy details to understand what information is collected and how documents are handled.
  • Compatibility: make sure your device runs an iOS version the listing supports.
  • User feedback: read recent reviews and weigh them against the developer's own description.

Using electronic signatures responsibly

Electronic signatures are not accepted in every situation, and the rules can vary by document type and location.
