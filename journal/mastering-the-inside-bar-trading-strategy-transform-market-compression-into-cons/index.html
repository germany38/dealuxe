The Problem with Modern Retail Trading

Modern trading education is often flooded with conflicting indicators, complicated oscillator setups, and promises of instant wealth. Many beginners and intermediate traders jump from strategy to strategy, adding moving averages, RSI lines, and MACD histograms to their charts until they can barely see the price action underneath.

Price action purists, however, know a fundamental truth: indicators lag behind price. By the time an indicator signals a move, institutional players have often already secured their positions. To gain a true edge, traders must learn to read raw candlestick psychology—specifically, how the market transitions from expansion to compression, and how accumulated energy releases during a breakout.

What Is the Inside Bar Trading Strategy?

An inside bar is a two-candlestick price pattern where the secondary candle (the inside bar) is completely engulfed by the high and low range of the primary candle (the mother bar). Visually, it represents a period of pause, hesitation, and price compression. Markets cannot move in a straight line forever; after a strong impulse wave, prices often contract as buyers and sellers pause to evaluate market value.

While amateur traders see an inside bar as mere indecision, professional price action traders recognize it as a coiled spring. It shows that volatility is contracting and institutional orders are accumulating. When structured correctly, trading the breakout of an inside bar allows you to enter trades with tight stop-losses and high reward-to-risk ratios.
