Tipple Tours: A Practical Guide to Wine, Beer and Food Holidays

Disclosure: Dealux may earn a commission on purchases made through links on this page, at no extra cost to you.

Some holidays are built around a museum or a beach, but others are built around a glass, a brewery or a long lunch with a local cook. Tipple Tours sits in the second group, offering wine, beer and food adventures in destinations around the world. If you enjoy drinking and eating with a sense of curiosity, and you would rather learn something than simply tick off landmarks, this is the kind of travel worth a closer look.

What makes a food and drink holiday different

A standard city break often means following a guidebook from one sight to the next. A tasting-led trip tends to revolve around people: producers, winemakers, brewers and cooks who explain how their products are made and why they taste the way they do. The pace is usually slower, and the conversation is often the highlight. Before booking, think about whether you want a relaxed itinerary with plenty of free time or a packed schedule of visits and tastings. Both can be enjoyable, but they suit very different travellers.

Who small-group trips tend to suit

Tipple Tours describes its trips as small-group adventures, and that phrase is worth unpacking. Smaller groups usually mean more time with hosts, more opportunity to ask questions and a more sociable atmosphere, especially for solo travellers who would like company without a large coach tour. The trade-off is that smaller groups can have fewer places available, so booking early may matter for popular dates. If you prefer complete flexibility or private arrangements, check whether a group format fits your plans before committing.

What to check on the brand's website

Every tour operator presents its trips differently, so it helps to compare the details rather than relying on a headline. On the official site, look for clear information about what is included, such as tastings, meals, transfers and entry fees, and what you will need to pay separately. Check the group size stated for each trip, the dates on offer, and the cancellation and refund terms. If you have dietary requirements, mobility needs or a particular interest in one style of drink, look for a contact route so you can ask before you pay.

Questions to ask before you book

A few simple questions can save disappointment later. Ask what happens if the trip is cancelled by the operator, and what happens if you need to cancel yourself. Ask how travel between locations is arranged and whether you will need to make your own way to the starting point. Find out how much alcohol is typically served and whether there are non-alcoholic options, which matters if you are driving, if you are pregnant or if you simply prefer not to drink. It is also sensible to confirm your travel insurance covers activities involving alcohol and any local transport you will use.

Drinking on holiday should be enjoyable and sensible. Pace yourself, drink plenty of water, and follow local laws on alcohol and driving, particularly when visiting wineries or breweries.

Is it worth it?

Whether a food and drink adventure feels good value depends on what you want from a holiday. If you are interested in how wine, beer and regional food are made, and you enjoy meeting people who care about their craft, a small-group trip can offer something a standard package rarely does. If your priority is the lowest possible cost or total independence, a self-planned trip might suit you better. Compare the inclusions, the group size and the flexibility on offer, then decide which balance feels right.

When you are ready to look at upcoming trips and see what is on offer, visit the Tipple Tours website to read the full details for each destination and check the terms before you book.
