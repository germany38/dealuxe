The Real Problem: Every Scrum Course Was Built for Developers

When professionals feel lost during an agile transition, they often blame themselves. They worry they lack the competence to keep up. But the truth is simple: Your confusion is not a competence gap; it is a training gap.

Traditional Scrum courses found on platforms like Udemy, Coursera, or LinkedIn Learning are created with software engineers and programmers in mind. They focus on code sprints, software releases, bug tracking, and preparing students for expensive IT certifications like the PSM or CSM. If you are a marketing manager launching a multi-channel campaign, an HR business partner managing a hiring pipeline, or a healthcare administrator organizing patient flow, software examples make zero sense for your daily reality.

This mismatch leads directly to common workplace frustrations:

  • "I don't understand the jargon, and I am too embarrassed to ask my team."
  • "Every resource uses coding examples that have zero relevance to my work."
  • "I genuinely don't know what I am supposed to DO in these sprint meetings."
  • "They told me to write a user story, but I don't build apps or software products."
