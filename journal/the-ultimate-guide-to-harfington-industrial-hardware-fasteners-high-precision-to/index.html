3. Material Specifications: Why Metallurgy & Quality Assurance Matter

When buying hardware online, material grade dictates performance under pressure. Harfington transparently lists physical material properties so engineers and builders can select components matched precisely to their environment:

304 & 316 Stainless Steel

Offers exceptional resistance to atmospheric rust and chemical corrosion. Grade 316 is ideal for marine environments, chemical handling, and outdoor structural installations exposed to salt spray.

High-Tensile Carbon & Alloy Steel

Heat-treated carbon steel provides maximum yield strength and shear resistance, making it suitable for automotive suspensions, heavy structural machinery, and high-stress mechanical joints.

Solid Brass & Copper Alloys

Non-magnetic with high electrical conductivity and anti-galling properties. Excellent for electrical grounding terminals, decorative woodworking, and low-friction mechanical valves.

Nitrile Rubber (NBR) & Fluororubber

Engineered fluid seals designed to maintain mechanical elasticity when exposed to petroleum oils, synthetic lubricants, hydraulic fluids, and high temperature fluctuations.

5. Step-by-Step Guide: Selecting the Right Hardware for Your Specific Project

To ensure maximum structural integrity and long-term durability, follow this standard engineering checklist when ordering hardware from Harfington:

  1. Identify Operating Environment Conditions: Determine if your assembly will encounter moisture, outdoor weather, marine saltwater, high heat, or corrosive chemicals. Select 316 Stainless Steel for corrosive exposures and zinc-plated carbon steel for dry indoor structural framing.
  2. Determine Load Dynamics & Tensile Requirements: Calculate whether the joint experiences static weight, dynamic shear, or intense vibration. Use lock nuts, spring washers, or thread-locking fluid when securing moving assemblies or motorized equipment.
  3. Verify Exact Thread Pitch & Diameter: Measure broken or replacement screws using a digital caliper or thread gauge to distinguish between Fine Metric (e.g., M8x1.0), Coarse Metric (e.g., M8x1.25), and SAE UNC/UNF threads.
  4. Choose the Right Head Type & Drive System: Select Socket Cap or Torx heads for high-torque applications with recessed space, Countersunk/Flat heads for flush finished surfaces, and External Hex heads for easy wrench access.
