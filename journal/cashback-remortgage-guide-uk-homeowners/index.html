Cashback Remortgage: What to Know Before You Switch Your Mortgage

When your fixed-rate deal is coming to an end, your lender will usually write to you with a standard variable rate that is often higher than what is available elsewhere. That is when many UK homeowners start looking at remortgaging. Cashback Remortgage is one option to explore, offering a comparison service for people who want to move their mortgage to a new deal and possibly receive cashback along the way.

What the service does

Cashback Remortgage compares the whole mortgage market rather than a limited panel of lenders. For a homeowner, that matters because the best-value product for your circumstances may come from a lender you would never have found by searching a single bank's website. A wider search gives you more products to weigh up, although it also means you will need to be clear about what you actually want from a new deal before you start.

It is worth remembering that remortgaging is not the same as taking out a brand-new loan. Your property is security for the debt, so the decisions you make have long-term consequences. Treat any comparison as a starting point for your own research rather than a finished answer.

Key features to look at

The brand highlights no broker fees and cashback of up to £1,500 on eligible completions. A no-fee service can make a real difference to the overall cost of switching, but always check the small print. Cashback is typically tied to specific conditions, so confirm which products qualify, when the payment is made and whether any minimum requirements apply. Ask yourself whether a cashback figure is more valuable to you than a slightly lower interest rate, because the two rarely come as a package.

What to compare beyond the headline offer

Cashback is only one part of the picture. When you compare remortgage deals, consider the following:

  • The interest rate and whether it is fixed, tracker or variable
  • The length of the deal and what happens when it ends
  • Arrangement or product fees, which may be added to the loan or paid upfront
  • Early repayment charges if you may sell or move before the deal finishes
  • Legal and valuation costs, and whether any are included
  • The total amount you will pay over the period you are likely to keep the loan

A simple way to test a deal is to calculate the total cost over two or three years, not just the first monthly payment. A cashback sum can look attractive, yet a higher rate or large fee could reduce the benefit over time.

Who this type of service may suit

A remortgage comparison service may be useful if you are approaching the end of a fixed deal, want to reduce your monthly payments, or are looking to release some equity for home improvements. It may also suit people who are comfortable reading documents and asking questions. If you have a complex income, a previous credit issue, or an unusual property, you may benefit from speaking to a regulated adviser before committing.

Mortgages are a significant financial commitment, and this is not personal financial advice. Only borrow what you can comfortably repay, and check that any product meets your needs before you sign.

Next steps

If you are considering a switch, gather your current mortgage statement, your latest lender letter, and a rough figure for your property's value. Then visit the Cashback Remortgage website to see what information it asks for and how its comparison works. Take your time, compare the total cost of each option, and make sure the deal you choose fits your plans for the years ahead.
