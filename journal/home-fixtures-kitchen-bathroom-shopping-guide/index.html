Home Fixtures: Shopping Guide for Kitchen and Bathroom Products

Disclosure: Dealux may earn a commission on purchases made through links on this page, at no extra cost to you.

Replacing a worn tap or planning a new shower room can feel like a bigger job than it needs to be. Home Fixtures is a UK-focused range of kitchen, bathroom, shower and plumbing products, so it is a sensible place to start when you are pricing up a project or simply need one replacement part. This guide explains how to narrow down your options and what to check before you order.

What the range covers

Home improvement products are easier to compare when you know how they fit together. Kitchen items, bathroom fittings, shower components and plumbing parts all serve different jobs, even though they often need to work as a system. A new mixer tap, for example, still depends on the water supply, the sink and the waste connection underneath.

If you are not sure what you need, begin with the room or the fault. Browsing by room is useful for a full refit, while searching by product type suits a single repair. Either way, make a short list of the items you have in mind so you can compare like with like.

Matching fixtures to your space

Measurements matter more than most shoppers expect. Before you buy, measure the space, the existing fixing holes and, where relevant, the centre-to-centre distance for taps or the depth available for a wall-mounted unit. For showers, check whether you have a bath, a tray or an open wet room, and note the water pressure in your home if you know it.

Plumbing parts need extra care. Confirm pipe sizes, connection types and whether you have a combi boiler, a gravity system or a pumped setup. Check the product details on the Home Fixtures website for dimensions and fitting information before you add anything to your basket. If something is unclear, a qualified plumber or installer can confirm compatibility, especially for work that involves gas, electrics or main water connections.

Comparing quality without the jargon

Quality is easier to judge when you break it down into a few practical points. Look at the finish, the materials, the moving parts and the information given about care and cleaning. A matt finish may suit a busy family kitchen, while a polished chrome might show water marks more quickly, so think about how the product will be used day to day.

  • The materials and finish listed on the product page
  • Dimensions, fixing requirements and included parts
  • Any warranty or guarantee terms stated on the site
  • Care instructions and recommended cleaning products

Be wary of comparing products on price alone. A cheaper part that needs replacing after a year may cost more in the long run than a well-specified option, so weigh the total cost of ownership rather than the sticker price.

Planning delivery, returns and aftercare

Before you check out, read the delivery information and returns policy on the website. Bulky items such as baths, basins or shower enclosures can take longer to arrive and may need someone at home to receive them. Keep your order confirmation, invoice and any packaging until you are sure the item works as expected, as you will usually need these if you want to return or claim under a guarantee.

It is also worth ordering a little extra sealant, washers or fixings where appropriate, so a small mistake does not hold up the whole job.

Who the range suits

Home Fixtures is likely to suit homeowners planning a refresh, renters replacing a tired fitting, and DIY enthusiasts who want a clear selection of kitchen and bathroom products in one place. Tradespeople sourcing parts for clients may also find it useful, provided they check specifications carefully for each job.

Whoever you are, a little preparation goes a long way. Measure twice, compare the details and read the terms before you buy.

When you are ready to look at the range, visit Home Fixtures and use the guide above to shortlist the products that fit your home, your budget and your plans.
