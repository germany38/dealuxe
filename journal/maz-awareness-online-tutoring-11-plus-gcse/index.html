Choosing support for a child preparing for the 11+ or GCSE exams can feel like a big decision, especially when so many options are available online. MAZ Awareness Affiliate offers UK online one-to-one tutoring aimed at exam success at both levels, so it is worth a closer look if you are weighing up home-based support. Here is a practical guide to what to consider and how to compare it with other choices.

Who one-to-one online tutoring tends to suit

One-to-one lessons give a pupil the full attention of a tutor, which can be useful when a child needs to work through specific gaps rather than keep pace with a whole class. Online delivery also saves travel time and makes it easier to fit sessions around school, clubs and family commitments. It tends to work best for pupils who are comfortable learning through a screen and for parents who can help keep a regular routine going.

Understanding the 11+ and GCSE focus

Preparation for the 11+ and for GCSEs is quite different. Entrance-style papers often test reasoning and verbal skills alongside core subjects, while GCSE revision follows the specification of the exam board your child is entered with. When you look at any tutoring service, check whether its lessons are clearly organised around the stage your child is at, rather than a general catch-all approach.

What to compare before you book

  • Whether tutors work with your child's year group and the relevant exam board
  • How each lesson is structured, and whether you receive feedback on progress
  • Session length, scheduling flexibility and how missed or cancelled lessons are handled
  • Clear information on pricing and any introductory options, checked directly on the provider's website
  • How the tutor keeps parents informed between sessions

Comparing these points side by side will tell you far more than any single headline feature. Make a short list of questions and put them to each provider before you commit.

Checking the details on the official site

Before making a decision, visit the official MAZ Awareness Affiliate website and read the service information carefully. Look for details on the subjects covered, how lessons take place and what support is included. Treat the site as your starting point, and compare what it says with what your family actually needs. If anything is unclear, it is reasonable to ask for clarification before signing up.

To get the most from any online tutoring, pick a quiet space with a reliable device and a stable connection, keep sessions short and regular, and review the work together at the end of each week. Small, consistent habits often matter more than the number of hours booked.

If one-to-one online tutoring for the 11+ or GCSE sounds like a good fit, take a look at what MAZ offers and compare it against your own checklist. Being organised and asking the right questions will help you make a confident choice for your child.
