Heard Review: Music Industry Contacts for Independent Artists

Getting your music in front of the right people is one of the hardest parts of being an independent artist. Heard is a US-based directory that gives musicians access to more than 10,000 music-industry contacts, including playlist curators, record labels, music blogs, and radio stations. It is built for artists and small teams who want a practical starting point for outreach without building a list from scratch.

Who Heard is best suited for

Heard is most relevant for independent artists, bedroom producers, small labels, and managers who already release music and need to reach people who can review, feature, or play it. If you are still writing songs and have not yet started promoting, a contact list will not do much on its own. It works best alongside a release plan, a press kit, and a clear idea of who your music is for.

Before deciding, think about your genre and your goals. A list that covers many categories is useful if you are reaching out across several channels, but you will still need to filter contacts to find the ones who genuinely cover your style.

What the contact list covers

According to the brand, the directory includes curators, labels, blogs, radio, and other industry contacts. When you look at any database like this, check how the entries are organized. Can you filter by category or genre? Does each entry include enough context, such as a website or submission guidelines, so you can understand what the contact actually accepts? A long list is only helpful if you can narrow it to a manageable group of relevant people.

Heard describes its contacts as verified. Verification standards vary across providers, so it is worth checking the brand's website for details on how entries are maintained and how often they are updated. Contacts change jobs, close blogs, and shift their submission rules, so ask how outdated information is handled.

Pricing and the free trial

Heard lists an annual price of $19, and it offers free contacts so you can try the service before committing. That structure lets you test the quality of the entries against your own needs. Pick a few genres or outlets you already know, check whether the free contacts match them, and see whether the information is accurate and usable.

To judge whether the price makes sense, compare it with the time you would otherwise spend researching contacts by hand. Also consider any other promotion tools you already pay for. You can review the current pricing and trial details directly on the Heard website, since prices and terms can change.

Using contacts responsibly

A contact list is a starting point, not a guarantee of coverage or placement. Most curators and editors receive large volumes of pitches, so personalized outreach tends to work better than mass emails. Mention a specific track or playlist, explain why your song fits, and keep your message short. Respect any submission guidelines and remove people who ask not to be contacted.

If you email contacts, make sure your practices follow the rules that apply where you and your recipients are located. In the US, that includes requirements under the CAN-SPAM Act, such as a clear sender identity and a working way to opt out. If your audience includes people in Europe or elsewhere, check local privacy rules as well.

A quick checklist before you buy

  • Does the directory cover the outlet types you actually need?
  • Can you filter contacts by genre, region, or category?
  • Are the free contacts accurate and relevant to your music?
  • Does the annual cost fit your release schedule and budget?
  • Do you have a plan for personalized, respectful outreach?

If you can answer yes to most of these, Heard may be worth a closer look. Visit the Heard site to test the free contacts and compare the details for yourself.
