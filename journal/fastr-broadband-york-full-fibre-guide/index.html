A slow connection can turn a simple video call into a frustrating experience, so choosing the right home broadband matters more than many people realise. Fastr Broadband is an independent full fibre internet service provider based in York, and it may be worth a look if you are reviewing your options. You can find out more on the Fastr Broadband website.

What full fibre means for your home

Full fibre refers to a network where fibre-optic cable runs all the way to the property, rather than switching to older copper wiring for part of the journey. Fastr says it runs its own network, which reaches over 10,000 premises, and it advertises speeds of up to 8Gbps. It is worth remembering that a top-end figure is the maximum available on a given package, not a guaranteed speed in every home. Your actual experience will depend on the plan you pick, the equipment you use and how your rooms are set up.

Check availability before you compare

The first practical step is to confirm that the service is available at your address. Look on the brand's website for a way to check coverage using your postcode. If your property is outside the network, the rest of the comparison becomes irrelevant, so it is a sensible place to start.

Match the speed to how you use the internet

Very high speeds sound appealing, but many households do not need the highest tier. Consider how your home actually uses the internet:

  • How many people are online at the same time, and for what purposes
  • Whether you do regular video calls, online gaming or large file uploads
  • How many smart devices, cameras or streaming screens are connected
  • Whether you need strong upload speeds as well as download speeds

Also remember that your router and Wi-Fi setup can limit what you receive inside the home. An older router or a poorly placed access point can hold back even a fast line, so it may be worth reviewing your equipment before upgrading your package.

Read the small print before you sign up

Headline monthly prices rarely tell the full story. When comparing any provider, check the length of the contract, whether there are setup or installation fees, how long installation takes, and what the monthly price will be once any introductory period ends. It is also useful to look at what support is available if something goes wrong and how easy it is to leave at the end of the term. A simple way to compare is to work out the total cost over 12 months for each option rather than focusing on the first month alone.

Who might consider Fastr Broadband

An independent provider with its own full fibre network and a local base in York may appeal to people who prefer a smaller, community-focused company. Households looking for a dependable connection for work and entertainment, and small businesses that want to compare fibre options, could both find it relevant. If you run a business, check the website for details of the packages designed for business use, as needs differ from those of a typical home.

Whatever you choose, take a few minutes to compare your options against your real usage, not just the highest headline speed. For a clear view of what is on offer, visit Fastr Broadband and review the current details directly on the site before making a decision.
