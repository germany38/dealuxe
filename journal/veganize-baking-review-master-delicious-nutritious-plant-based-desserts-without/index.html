Why Traditional Vegan Baking Fails (And How to Fix It)

When most people first transition to veganism, they try to directly substitute plant-based ingredients into old family recipes without understanding the chemical roles that eggs and dairy play in baking. Eggs provide structure, moisture, binding power, and lift. Butter adds rich fat, tender crumb structure, and specific melting points. Milk provides moisture, proteins, and browning sugars.

Simply swapping almond milk for cow's milk or coconut oil for butter without adjusting ratios or techniques leads directly to common kitchen disasters:

    - **Dry and Crumbling Cakes:** Without the correct protein and fat emulsions, sponge cakes lose their structural integrity. - **Rock-Hard Cookies:** Using improper binding alternatives turns sweet treats into weapons instead of delicious desserts. - **Dense, Heavy Breads:** Lacking proper yeast activation and moisture retention, breads become thick and unappetizing.

To succeed, you have to completely reset your mindset. You cannot just replicate traditional recipes blindly—you need a tested blueprint designed specifically for plant-based chemistry.
