ReachStream Review: A Practical Guide to B2B Prospecting Data

Disclosure: Dealux may earn a commission on purchases made through links on this page, at no extra cost to you.

Finding the right customers is one of the hardest parts of selling to other businesses. ReachStream is a B2B prospecting data platform designed to help sales and marketing teams identify and reach the companies and decision-makers they want to work with. Here is a practical look at how it works and how to decide whether it fits your workflow.

How the targeting filters work

ReachStream lets you narrow a prospect list using criteria such as job title, industry, company name, technology stack, employee size, revenue, and location. Used together, these filters can move you from a broad market to a shortlist that matches a specific type of buyer. The key is to treat each filter as an answer to a real question: Who makes the purchasing decision? What kind of company needs what you sell? Which tools does a prospect already use?

Building a clear ideal customer profile

Before you run any search, write down what your best customers have in common. A simple profile makes filtering faster and keeps your outreach focused. Consider the following when you define yours:

  • The job titles of the people who influence the buying decision, not only the final signer
  • The industries where your product solves a clear problem
  • A company size range your team can realistically serve
  • Technology signals that show a prospect is a good fit for your offer
  • Geographic areas where you can meet, call, or support customers

Testing your profile on a small sample first is wise. If the results look too broad or too narrow, adjust one filter at a time so you can see what changed.

Who it tends to suit

A platform like this is most useful for teams that already run outbound sales or account-based marketing and need a steady source of prospect lists. Sales development representatives, business development staff, and small agencies that prospect on behalf of clients often look for this kind of tool. Solo founders may find it helpful too, though they should be realistic about the time needed to research and personalize outreach. If your business relies mainly on referrals or inbound leads, a prospecting database may be a secondary tool rather than a core one.

To see which filters are currently offered and how the interface is laid out, review the ReachStream website directly rather than relying on third-party summaries, which can be outdated.

What to check before you commit

Data quality is the first question to ask about any prospecting tool. Look for information on how contact data is sourced, how often it is updated, and what happens when a record turns out to be wrong. Ask about data use as well. In the United States, email outreach is shaped by rules such as the CAN-SPAM Act, and state privacy laws may affect how personal information is collected and used. Make sure your campaigns include opt-out options and honor unsubscribe requests, whichever tool you use.

Also check whether the site offers a demo or trial period. If it does, test the platform with a real target list and measure how many results match your profile before you pay for anything. Compare it against other prospecting tools on the same criteria: filter depth, ease of use, export options, and support.

Remember that a database only gives you names and details. Results still depend on how well you research each account, how relevant your message is, and how consistently you follow up.

The bottom line

ReachStream offers a focused set of targeting criteria for teams that want to build prospect lists with more precision. If you have a defined ideal customer profile and a process for outreach, it is worth exploring. Start by mapping your criteria, then visit ReachStream to see whether its filters match the way your team sells.
